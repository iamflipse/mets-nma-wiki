# CNMA Additivity: Do Combined Effects Equal the Sum of Parts?

The review's key methodological question: when a combined exercise–dietary intervention is decomposed into its exercise and diet components, does the observed combined effect equal the **sum of the component effects**?

## Results by outcome

| Outcome | Additivity test (Q-statistic, Holm–Bonferroni) |
|---|---|
| **WC** | ✅ Compatible — combined-intervention estimates matched the sum of component estimates (e.g. CR −2.93/−2.54/−7.05/−5.89/−2.87/−1.76 cm by node). "This finding does not establish synergy or superiority." |
| **TG** | ✅ No statistically significant departure from additivity (global Q-test). Component 95% CIs excluded 0 for MICT (−10.38, CI −14.94 to −5.82 mg/dL) and HIIT (−8.07, CI −15.59 to −0.55 mg/dL). |
| **SBP, DBP, FBG, HDL-C** | ❌ Additivity assumption **formally rejected** (all Q P < 0.001) → component-level interpretation not valid for these outcomes. |

**Caveats:**
- The auxiliary CNMA was restricted to the **additive** specification; **interaction terms were not estimated**, so model misfit for the 4 rejected outcomes indicates non-additivity but does not identify *which* component combinations are synergistic or antagonistic.
- Combined-intervention nodes were sparse (often k = 1–4 studies), so the additivity tests for WC/TG rest on limited direct evidence.
