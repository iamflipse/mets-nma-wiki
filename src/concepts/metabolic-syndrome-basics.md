# Metabolic Syndrome: The 6 Components

**Metabolic syndrome (MetS)** is a cluster of cardiometabolic risk factors that travel together and independently raise the risk of cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, and premature mortality. The diagnostic components this review tracks as primary outcomes:

| Component | Abbrev. | Direction favoring benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Waist circumference | WC | ↓ |
| Systolic blood pressure | SBP | ↓ |
| Diastolic blood pressure | DBP | ↓ |
| Fasting blood glucose | FBG | ↓ |
| Triglycerides | TG | ↓ |
| HDL-cholesterol | HDL-C | ↑ (the only component where *higher* is better) |

**Target population:** adults (≥18 y) with MetS **and** overweight/obesity (BMI ≥25 kg/m²) — excess adiposity is a major modifiable contributor to the cardiometabolic abnormalities of MetS, so weight management is a central therapeutic target.

**Why component-specific comparisons matter:** a treatment can improve one diagnostic component while leaving others unchanged, so an apparent "overall benefit" can mask clinically important outcome-specific differences. This is why the review compares each intervention node against each of the 6 outcomes separately rather than a single composite.

**Lifestyle modification** is the foundation of MetS management per major professional societies. Structured exercise-only interventions vary in methods, intensity, volume, supervision, and progression; dietary-only interventions vary in energy targets, macronutrient composition, and pattern. The 8 dietary nodes are grouped descriptively as:

- **Energy-deficit strategies:** caloric restriction (CR), intermittent fasting (IF)
- **Single-macronutrient approaches:** low-carbohydrate diet (LCD), high-protein diet (HPD), low-fat diet (LFD)
- **Whole-diet pattern interventions:** Mediterranean diet (MedDiet), DASH, general dietary counseling (GDC)

**Exercise nodes:** MICT (moderate-intensity continuous training), HIIT (high-intensity interval training), RT (resistance training), COMB-EX (combined aerobic + resistance).
